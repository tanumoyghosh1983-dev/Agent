Given the page offers hourly pricing, something like 'Get My Free VA Quote' or 'Hire a VA — Start Free Trial' would set expectation and reduce click hesitation. The secondary CTA 'Submit Your Requirement' is clearer but generic and asks for effort ('requirement') before any value is promised. No risk-reversal microcopy near the buttons even though a 100% money-back guarantee exists further down the page.
Message Match 4/5H1 'Hire Virtual Assistants From India' plus the explicit rate band 'From US$4.5/Hr to US$9/Hr' is unusually specific and should match high-intent queries like 'hire virtual assistant India' or 'virtual assistant hourly rate' very well. The three proof bullets (150+ VAs, college-educated, multi-skill) reinforce it. Points lost for the sub-headline opening with 'Wait!' — an exit-intent/pop-up voice that reads as desperate on a paid-click entry page — and for the absence of any offer framing (no free trial, no first-week discount, no 'no contract') that would differentiate from the dozens of identical India-VA competitors bidding on the same terms.

Section backgrounds alternate light/dark, which helps, but there is no visual emphasis on the money-back guarantee or the pricing after the hero.
